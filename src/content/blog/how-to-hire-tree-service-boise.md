## Insurance: How to Actually Verify It

Everyone says they're insured. The verification is the part that separates claims from reality.

**Ask for a certificate of insurance sent directly from the insurance agency**, addressed to you, not a photograph of a document handed over on a phone. A certificate that arrives from the insurer confirms the policy exists and is current on the day of your job. A PDF forwarded by the contractor confirms a PDF exists.

**Carry a $2 million minimum on general liability.** Tree work involves heavy weight moving at height near structures, and lower limits are common but thin. If a 60-foot cottonwood goes the wrong way, small policies get exhausted quickly.

**Verify workers' compensation separately.** This is the one homeowners skip and the one that hurts most. If a climber falls in your yard and there's no workers' comp behind him, the path to recovery can run through your property and your policy. Be skeptical of any outfit that explains its whole crew away as independent contractors.

**Confirm the coverage matches the work.** Some policies exclude crane operations or work above a certain height. If your job needs a crane, say so when you ask, and see our guide to [crane tree removal costs in Boise](/blog/crane-tree-removal-boise-cost/) for when that's the right method.

## Credentials Worth Checking

**ISA certification.** The International Society of Arboriculture certifies individual arborists, not companies, so ask which certified arborist will be on your job and verify them in the [ISA directory](https://www.isa-arbor.com/). "We're ISA-certified" without a name to check is a marketing sentence.

**The right credential for the job.** Routine removal and trimming need a competent, insured crew. A diagnosis of whether a tree can be saved needs an arborist, which is what a [tree health assessment](/blog/tree-health-assessment-boise/) at **$75 to $250** buys. Something that will be read by a city permit office, an insurer, or a court needs a formal document, covered in [when you need an arborist report](/blog/arborist-report-when-you-need-one-boise/).

**Local presence.** A verifiable address and a phone number that reaches a person. After storms, the Treasure Valley fills with out-of-area crews who will not be here in six months if something goes wrong.

## What a Real Quote Contains

Get it in writing, and make sure it names all four of these:

**1. Which trees, specifically.** Species and location, not "the trees in back." Ambiguity here is where change orders come from.

**2. The exact scope.** Removal to the ground, or removal plus stump? Trimming what percentage, and by what method? "Trim the maple" can mean a **$350** visit or a **$900** one.

**3. Cleanup and debris.** Is the wood hauled away, chipped and left, cut to firewood lengths and stacked, or your problem? This is the single most common surprise on a tree invoice. Our guides on [what to do with wood after removal](/blog/what-to-do-with-wood-after-removal/) and [tree debris disposal in Boise](/blog/tree-debris-disposal-boise/) cover the options and what each is worth.

**4. The stump.** Grinding is usually a separate line, running **$150 to $500** as an add-on to a removal, and grinding depth should be stated if you plan to replant. [What to plant after a tree removal](/blog/what-to-plant-after-tree-removal/) covers why depth matters.

Also confirm access and property protection: where the truck parks, whether the lawn will be driven on, how the sprinkler system and fences are protected, and who repairs damage if it happens.

## Red Flags

**They knocked on your door after a storm.** The single strongest predictor of a bad outcome. [Storm chaser scams in Boise](/blog/tree-service-storm-chaser-scams-boise/) covers the pattern in detail: urgency, a discount that expires today, a large cash deposit, and a crew that leaves mid-job.

**They want a large deposit up front.** Routine residential tree work does not require substantial prepayment. Cash-only with no invoice is worse.

**They recommend topping.** Cutting a tree's crown back to stubs is not pruning, it permanently damages the tree, and it creates weak regrowth that becomes a hazard in a few years. [Why tree topping is bad](/blog/tree-topping-bad-boise/) covers it. A company that offers topping as a service is telling you what they know.

**They quote a street tree without mentioning the city.** Right-of-way trees are regulated, and a crew that doesn't raise that isn't paying attention. See [tree removal permits in Boise](/blog/tree-removal-permits-boise/).

**They offer to work near power lines.** Vegetation on distribution lines is Idaho Power's work. See [trees near power lines](/blog/tree-near-power-lines-boise/).

**No written anything.** If the whole arrangement lives in a conversation, you have nothing when it goes sideways.

**Pressure to decide immediately.** Real hazards do exist and sometimes the urgency is genuine. The test is whether they'll put the reason in writing and let you get a second opinion. A **$75 to $250** assessment is cheap insurance against a **$3,000** removal you didn't need.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What insurance should a tree service carry in Boise?

General liability with a **$2 million minimum**, plus workers' compensation for the crew. Ask for the certificate of insurance to be sent to you directly by the insurance agency rather than handed over by the contractor, and confirm the policy covers the specific work, since some exclude crane operations or height limits.

### How do I verify an arborist is really ISA-certified?

Ask for the individual arborist's name and look them up in the International Society of Arboriculture's public directory. Certification belongs to people, not companies, so a company-level claim with no name attached can't be checked.

### How many quotes should I get for tree removal?

Two or three for anything substantial, given the same scope so the numbers are comparable. For a small ornamental trim at **$150 to $400** it isn't worth the time; for a large removal at **$2,500 to $5,000+** it certainly is.

### Is it normal to pay a deposit for tree work?

For routine residential removal and trimming, little or no deposit is normal. Large deposits, particularly cash without an invoice, are a common feature of storm-chasing operations. Substantial mobilization costs, like a crane, can justify a partial deposit with a written agreement.

### Why is one tree removal quote so much cheaper than the others?

Usually because something isn't in it. The common omissions are insurance, debris haul-away, stump grinding, and the experience to price a difficult drop zone correctly. Ask what the low bid includes line by line before treating it as the same job.
