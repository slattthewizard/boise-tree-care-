Tight access tree removal in Boise usually costs more than the same tree would on an open lot, often landing near the top of its size tier instead of the middle, because a crew that can't get a chipper or a loader to the trunk has to do everything by hand. The narrower the path in (a North End alley, a fenced backyard, a 32-inch side gate) the more labor hours the job eats up, and labor is what drives price once the tree itself stops being the hard part.

That surprises homeowners who assume price is set purely by height and species. Two identical 50-foot trees can land in different spots on the same pricing tier depending on whether a chipper can park ten feet away or a crew has to hand-carry every log around the house instead.

## Why Tight Access Tree Removal in Boise Costs More Than the Tree Itself

Our [tree removal page](/tree-removal-boise/) prices by size: $300-$800 for a small tree under 30 feet, $800-$2,500 for a medium tree, and $2,500-$5,000+ for a large one over 60 feet. That range assumes a crew can bring a chipper, and maybe a small loader, within reach of the trunk. Take that access away and the tree still comes down the same way, cut in sections from the top down, but now each section has to be carried, dragged, or wheelbarrowed to wherever the chipper or truck can actually sit. On a cramped lot that can mean forty feet of hauling through a side yard instead of ten feet to a chipper in the driveway.

That extra handling is where the cost really lives. It isn't a markup for inconvenience, it's paying for the extra hours a two or three person crew spends moving wood instead of cutting it. Stump grinding has the same issue in miniature: a grinder that would normally roll straight to the stump has to be walked in through whatever gap exists, turning a $75-$150 small stump into a job that simply takes longer.

Hiring the right crew matters more here too, since a crew used to open lots can misjudge a tight job, either overbidding out of caution or underbidding and hitting a change order midway. ## North End Alleys and Boise's Older Lots

The North End is the textbook example of what tight access does to a bid. Lots there were platted decades before anyone thought about chipper trucks or skid steers, so setbacks are narrow and side yards are sometimes barely wide enough to walk through. Plenty of properties back onto a shared alley instead of a full-width street, and that alley is a genuine advantage: a truck staged there cuts the hand-carry distance versus a lot with no rear access, which is why alley-served lots often price toward the bottom of their tier rather than the top.

A homeowner on a narrow North End side street ran into the other side of that equation. A mature silver maple sat between a detached garage and a property line fence, with a 34-inch gate on the only open side. No truck could reach the backyard. The crew hand-carried every section down the side yard to a chipper staged in the alley, and the final bill came to about $4,100, near the top of the $2,500-$5,000+ tier for that size tree instead of the middle of it.

Older North End lots also carry a permit wrinkle worth knowing before you schedule anything. Boise has a street-tree rule for anything growing between the sidewalk and the curb, and a fair number of North End trees sit right in that strip, so removal there is never automatically permit-free. Check our guide to [Boise's tree removal permit rules](/blog/tree-removal-permits-boise/) and the [City of Boise's](https://www.cityofboise.org) urban forestry information before you assume a parkway tree is yours to cut without a step first.

## Gate Width: The Measurement That Decides Your Estimate

If you take one thing from this article, measure your gate before you call. That single number tells a crew more about your job than the tree's height does. Most skid steers and mini loaders need at least 36 inches of clear width. A walk-behind stump grinder can squeeze through around 30 to 32 inches. A chipper truck needs closer to a full driveway lane, 8 feet or more, which is why chippers almost always stay at the curb rather than entering a backyard at all.

Standard gates in newer Boise neighborhoods run 36 to 42 inches, fitting most equipment with room to spare. Plenty of older fences around the North End, the Bench, and older Garden City blocks run narrower, sometimes 28 to 32 inches, since nobody building a fence decades ago planned around a skid steer. Under that threshold, a crew's options are hand-carry everything or pull a fence panel to widen the opening temporarily, both of which add labor hours to the estimate.

## Crane vs. Hand-Carry: Which One Actually Costs Less

For a genuinely boxed-in tree, hand-carry isn't always the cheaper option, even though it sounds like the fallback. Once a large tree has zero equipment access and every section has to go out by hand, labor hours can pile up fast enough that a crane, mobilization fee and all, ends up costing about the same or less for a single afternoon of lifting.

A homeowner in Garden City ran into exactly that trade-off. A 65-foot cottonwood leaned toward the roof of a detached garage, boxed in by a six-foot privacy fence with no gate wider than 30 inches anywhere on the lot. Hand-carrying that volume of wood out over several days was estimated at close to $6,500 in labor alone. Bringing in a crane for a half-day lift instead, setting the boom over the fence line and lowering sections onto a trailer at the curb, brought the total to about $5,800, near the bottom of the $5,000-$8,000+ extreme and crane tier and less than the hand-carry number it replaced. Our [Garden City tree service page](/tree-service-garden-city/) has more on what's typical for that area's older, tighter lots.

Crane work isn't automatic, though. The boom needs a clear overhead path, so if service lines cross the yard, expect extra coordination before the lift; no crew touches the wires themselves, that stays [Idaho Power's](https://www.idahopower.com) territory on distribution lines. A fenced yard with a tree leaning toward the house is also a poor candidate for DIY: chainsaw work above shoulder height, anything near a power line, any climbing, or a tree that could reach a structure calls for a professional instead.
