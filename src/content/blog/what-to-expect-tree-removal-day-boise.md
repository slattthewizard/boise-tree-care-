The two things that determine how the day goes are access and the drop zone, and both were assessed when you got your quote.

## Before the Day: What Was Already Settled

By the time a crew is scheduled, the estimate visit should have established:

- Which tree, and whether it's coming down entirely or being reduced
- What's underneath it and how the pieces will be brought down
- Where the truck and chipper will sit
- Whether a crane is needed, covered in [crane tree removal costs](/blog/crane-tree-removal-boise-cost/)
- What happens to the wood and debris
- Whether the stump is included
- Any permit or approval questions, from [tree removal permits in Boise](/blog/tree-removal-permits-boise/) to HOA sign-off

If any of those are still open the day before, call and settle them. Ambiguity on removal day is what produces change orders. [How to hire a tree service in Boise](/blog/how-to-hire-tree-service-boise/) covers what a written quote should name.

## The Five Things to Do the Night Before

**1. Clear the drop zone and the work area.** Patio furniture, planters, grills, garden ornaments, vehicles, kids' toys, anything fragile within reach of the tree. Crews will move what they can, but things get missed and nothing in that zone is safe.

**2. Move vehicles out of the driveway.** The chipper and truck need it, and you don't want to be blocked in or covered in sawdust.

**3. Mark your sprinkler heads and shallow lines.** Flags or spray paint. Irrigation is the most commonly damaged thing on a removal day and it's the easiest to prevent.

**4. Unlock the gate, or arrange access.** A locked side gate on a back-yard removal costs the crew an hour or gets the job rescheduled. If the tree is only reachable through a neighbor's property, that conversation happens before the day, not during it.

**5. Plan for pets and kids.** Indoors, away from the work area, for the duration. Chainsaws, chippers, and falling wood are not a spectator situation, and gates get left open.

Worth adding on some properties: tell your neighbors. Not required, but a heads-up about a loud morning goes a long way, particularly if the crew needs to work near a shared fence.

## How the Day Actually Runs

**Arrival and setup.** The crew walks the job with you if you're home, confirms the scope against the written quote, and sets up: truck and chipper positioned, drop zone cleared and roped off, gear staged. This is the moment to raise anything that's changed since the estimate.

**The climb or the lift.** How the tree comes down depends on what's underneath it. On an open lot with room to fall, sections come down fast. Near a house, fence, or driveway, a climber works from the top down, cutting pieces and lowering them on ropes in a controlled sequence, which is slower and is most of what you're paying for. [Tight access removals](/blog/tight-access-tree-removal-boise/) covers the version where everything has to come out by hand through a side yard.

**Limbs first, then the trunk.** The canopy is stripped and fed to the chipper as it comes down. Once the tree is a bare spar, the trunk comes down in sections, sized so they can be moved or handled safely.

**Chipping.** Continuous through most of the day, and it's the loudest part. Brush becomes chips, which either go in the truck or stay on site if you asked for a chip drop.

**The stump.** If grinding is in the scope, it usually happens once the trunk is clear. Standard depth is six to twelve inches below grade. [Stump grinding versus stump removal](/blog/stump-grinding-vs-stump-removal-boise/) covers the difference and what to do with the hole afterward.

**Cleanup.** Debris loaded, area raked, driveway blown clear. Some sawdust and small debris in surrounding beds is normal and settles out.

**Walkthrough.** If you're home, walk it with the crew lead before they leave. This is the right moment to note anything that needs attention, not three days later.

## What About Your Lawn and Driveway

The honest answer: some impact is normal, significant damage is not.

**Expect** tire tracks on a lawn if equipment came across it, particularly in wet spring conditions. Expect some ruts to need raking and a bit of reseeding. Expect sawdust.

**Don't expect** deep ruts, broken irrigation, cracked driveway concrete, damaged fencing, or gouged bark on the trees you're keeping. Those are damage, they should be discussed and repaired, and they're one reason the insurance question matters when you hire.

**Ways to reduce impact:** schedule in dry conditions rather than after rain, or in winter when frozen ground carries equipment well; use plywood or mats over lawn where equipment has to cross; and confirm the parking and travel path during the estimate rather than on the day.

## Do You Need to Be Home?

Usually not, if the scope is agreed in writing, access is arranged, and someone can be reached by phone.

Be home if:

- The scope has any ambiguity, particularly on trimming rather than removal
- You want to keep specific wood or direct where chips go
- Access requires you to open something
- It's a large, complex, or crane job you'd like to see
- The tree is close to something you care about and you want to be reachable fast

If you can't be there, leave a number that will be answered and confirm the gate situation in advance.

## After the Crew Leaves

**Expect settling and a bit of debris.** Small chips and sawdust in beds and lawn work themselves in.

**Fill the stump hole properly** if grinding was done. Chips alone will sink; mix in topsoil. Details in the [stump grinding guide](/blog/stump-grinding-vs-stump-removal-boise/).

**Plan the replacement while you're thinking about it.** [What to plant after a tree removal](/blog/what-to-plant-after-tree-removal/) and [the best trees to plant in Boise](/blog/best-trees-to-plant-boise/) cover species that suit our alkaline soil, and fall is generally the better planting window here.

**Deal with the wood.** If you kept rounds for firewood, they need splitting and a season or two of seasoning. **Check the neighbors.** A quick look over the fence for stray debris is a two-minute courtesy that prevents a phone call.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does tree removal take?

Most residential removals take half a day. A large tree, difficult access, or a crane job can run a full day, and occasionally two. The variable is rarely the cutting itself; it's how the pieces have to be brought down and how far the debris has to be carried to the chipper.

### Do I need to be home when my tree is removed?

Usually not, provided the scope is agreed in writing, gates and parking are arranged, and you're reachable by phone. Be there if there's any ambiguity in the scope, if you want particular wood kept, or if access requires you to open something.

### Will tree removal damage my lawn?

Some impact is normal, especially in wet conditions: tire tracks, minor ruts, sawdust. Deep ruts, broken irrigation, or damaged hardscape are not normal and should be raised immediately. Marking sprinkler heads beforehand and scheduling in dry or frozen conditions both help.

### What happens to the tree after it's cut down?

Depends on what you agreed. The usual default is that everything is chipped and hauled away. You can also ask for the wood cut to firewood lengths and left, or for a chip drop to use as mulch. Confirm it in the written quote, since this is the most common source of surprise on a tree invoice.

### Do I need to move my car for tree removal?

Yes, out of the driveway and away from the work area. The truck and chipper generally need the driveway, and you don't want a vehicle parked under a work zone or blocked in for the day.
