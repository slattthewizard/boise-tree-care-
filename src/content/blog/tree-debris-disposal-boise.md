Which path makes sense, and what it costs, depends on volume, species, and who's doing the hauling.

## Tree Debris Disposal in Ada County: the Landfill and Hidden Hollow

Most Boise, Meridian, Eagle, and Garden City removals end up at the Ada County Landfill at 10300 N Seamans Gulch Rd on Boise's north side, open Monday through Saturday and closed Sundays. Longtime residents still call the whole property "Hidden Hollow," but that's technically the name of the landfill's original disposal cell, which closed years ago. The active cell today is called North Ravine; Hidden Hollow stuck around as the nickname everyone uses for the site as a whole, closed cell or not.

Not everything that arrives gets buried. Ada County runs a Recovery & Diversion Area that takes branches, trees, and brush (though not thorny debris or bamboo), along with clean wood like pallets, lumber, and plywood, though not stained, painted, burned, or pressure-treated wood. A landfill contractor chips the accepted material, pulls any metal out with a magnet, and sells the result as mulch or animal bedding instead of landfilling it. That's part of why a crew sorts debris on-site into separate loads instead of throwing everything into one truck.

## Nampa, Caldwell, and Canyon County's Pickles Butte Landfill

Jobs in Nampa and Caldwell generally route to Pickles Butte Sanitary Landfill, Canyon County's main disposal site at 15500 Missouri Ave in Nampa, open Monday through Saturday. Canyon County runs the same basic system as Ada County: a flat minimum charge for small loads, a per-ton rate above that, and a separate, generally simpler path for clean yard debris than for mixed household trash.

**A Caldwell homeowner clearing storm damage** hauled a full tree's worth of brush and a few downed limbs to Pickles Butte over a single weekend, splitting the load into two truckloads on the idea that a lighter load would cost less. It didn't work that way. Weight is weight no matter how many trips it takes, and the fuel and time across two trips added up to more than expected. Professional cleanup for a comparable medium tree, hauling included, typically runs $800 to $2,500 in one visit instead of a weekend of driving.

## How Landfill Fees Are Actually Structured

Both counties charge by weight, not volume, and both use the same basic shape: a flat minimum fee covers any load up to a certain weight, and a per-ton rate kicks in above that threshold. That structure is exactly why splitting a job into several small loads rarely saves money. You pay the minimum on every trip, whether the truck bed is a quarter full or packed to the rails.

Rates get revisited periodically at both counties, so treat any number you hear secondhand as a starting point, not a quote. [Ada County's landfill page](https://adacounty.id.gov/landfill/) publishes current fees and hours, and Canyon County posts the same for Pickles Butte. ## What Boise's Curbside Yard Waste Program Will and Won't Take

Boise's Curb It program picks up yard and garden waste weekly in a dedicated compost cart, and it takes leaves, grass clippings, weeds, and small tree trimmings and branches. Overflow that won't fit goes in paper leaf bags set next to the cart, not loose at the curb.

What the cart isn't built for is a whole tree. Even a modest removal produces more brush and chip volume in one day than a weekly cart is designed to move over several collection cycles, which is why full removal cleanup goes on a truck to the landfill or into a chip drop instead of trickling out to the curb. For routine trimming debris, the cart is usually all you need. The [City of Boise's yard waste page](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/public-works/curb-it/curb-it-sorting-guide/compost/yard-and-garden-waste/) covers what's accepted, or call Curb It directly at (208) 608-7136 with questions about a specific load.

## Chip Drops: the Middle Ground Between Hauling and Keeping It All

A chip drop means the crew leaves a load of fresh wood chips on your property instead of trucking it to the landfill, and it's usually free since it's material already on board. It works well if you have garden beds or bare soil to cover, and it means one less load at the scale house either way.

If you're also weighing firewood, milling a big trunk section, or the fuller breakdown of what happens to the parts of a tree you might want to keep, our guide on [what happens to the wood after tree removal](/blog/what-to-do-with-wood-after-removal/) walks through that decision in detail. This article is about the debris you're not keeping: where it ends up and what that trip actually costs. Large removals, especially a mature [cottonwood](/blog/cottonwood-tree-removal-boise/), can generate more brush and chip volume in one job than most properties have a use for, which is usually when a straight haul-away or a landfill trip makes more sense than trying to keep it all.

Stump material is its own separate story. Stump grindings are a mix of wood, dirt, and decayed material, not clean chips, and neither county's diversion program treats them the same as sorted brush. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where does tree debris actually end up in Boise?

Most of it goes to the Ada County Landfill north of the city, or Canyon County's Pickles Butte Landfill for Nampa and Caldwell jobs. Clean branches and unpainted wood often get diverted into a chipping program instead of buried, while mixed or contaminated debris goes to general disposal.

### What's the difference between "the landfill" and "Hidden Hollow"?

Hidden Hollow is technically the name of the Ada County Landfill's original, now-closed disposal cell, not the whole site. Most locals use it as a nickname for the entire landfill, including North Ravine, the cell that's actually active today.

### Will my curbside yard waste cart take tree removal debris?

For routine trimming, a few branches, leaves, and clippings, yes. For the volume from a full tree removal, no. A single removal typically produces more debris in one day than a weekly compost cart is sized to handle over several collection cycles.

### Does hauling debris myself actually save money compared to a professional removal?

Sometimes, but often less than expected. Landfills charge a flat minimum per load regardless of how full the truck is, so splitting a job into multiple small trips usually costs close to what a single professional haul-away would, once you count fuel and time.

### Does debris disposal work differently after a big windstorm?

The destinations don't change, but the volume does. When storm damage hits a lot of properties at once, landfill lines get longer and self-hauling becomes less practical, which is usually when scheduling a professional cleanup makes more sense than several personal trips.
