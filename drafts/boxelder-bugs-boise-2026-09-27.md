# Boxelder Bugs in Boise: Why They Swarm Your House, and Whether the Tree Has to Go

If black and red bugs are piling up on your south or west-facing wall every fall, you likely have a boxelder bug problem, not a structural one. These insects feed on the seeds of female, seed-bearing boxelder trees (and sometimes maple seeds), then head for the warmest surface they can find once the weather cools, which is usually the sunny side of your house.

The bugs themselves are harmless. They do not bite, do not carry disease, and do not damage the tree or your home. What they do is show up by the hundreds, stain light-colored siding, and smell bad when crushed. Cutting down the boxelder tree in your own yard sometimes helps, but it often will not solve the swarm on its own, because the bugs fly in from other trees too.

This guide covers why boxelder bugs pick your walls, whether they are actually hurting anything, what removing the tree does and does not fix, and what to do instead if the tree is not the real problem.

## What Boxelder Bugs Actually Are

Boxelder bugs are true bugs, not beetles, roughly half an inch long with a flattened black body and three narrow red or orange lines running down the back. Young nymphs are smaller, wingless, and solid red, which is why people sometimes mistake a fresh hatch for a different insect entirely. In the western United States, including Idaho, the species you are looking at is usually the western boxelder bug.

They are not a health threat. They do not bite people, sting, or spread disease indoors. The real complaints are cosmetic and behavioral: they gather in large numbers, leave small reddish-brown stains on curtains, siding, and window sills, and give off an unpleasant odor when squashed. [University of Idaho Extension](https://www.uidaho.edu/extension/publications/cis-1208) lists boxelder bugs as a common Idaho nuisance pest that can become numerous enough to alarm homeowners even though they are not doing any real harm. If you are seeing them in the hundreds on one wall, that is normal boxelder bug behavior, not a sign of an infestation spreading into something worse.

## Why They Swarm Your Walls Every Fall

Boxelder bugs spend the growing season feeding on the leaves, flowers, and developing seeds of female boxelder trees, and occasionally on other maple or ash seeds nearby. As temperatures drop in fall, the adults leave the tree and look for a dry, sheltered place to spend the winter. In nature that means rock piles, leaf litter, and hollow logs. Around a house, it means cracks in siding, gaps around windows and utility lines, and the wall voids behind them.

South and west-facing walls catch the most afternoon sun, so they warm up faster and stay warmer longer into the evening. That is the wall boxelder bugs pick to bask on before slipping into a gap for winter, sometimes in numbers that cover most of a wall on a warm fall afternoon. They are not trying to get into your living space specifically. They are following heat and shelter, and your house happens to offer both.

## Do Boxelder Bugs Harm the Tree or the House?

No. They feed on seeds and soft tissue in a way that does not damage a healthy boxelder tree, and they do not chew wood, bore into siding, or breed inside walls the way some other pests do. A boxelder bug problem is a nuisance, not a structural one, for both the tree and the house.

Where they do cause trouble is indoors, if enough of them get past your exterior seals. Large numbers inside a house can stain fabric and painted surfaces with waste and release a strong smell when crushed, which is why extension guidance recommends vacuuming them up rather than squashing them. None of that is damage in the sense of rot, pest tunneling, or weakened wood. ## Will Removing the Boxelder Tree Get Rid of Them?

Sometimes, partially. Removing a seed-bearing female boxelder cuts off the food source that draws bugs to your yard in the first place, and [Utah State University Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/ipm/structural-pest-id-guide/boxelder-bug) lists removing female boxelder trees as one of the more effective long-term management steps. But it comes with a real limit: adult boxelder bugs can fly for several blocks. If a neighbor has a female boxelder, or your street has maple trees the bugs will also feed on, removing your own tree will not stop the fall swarm entirely.

That makes tree removal a bigger, more expensive move for a bug problem by itself. Here is roughly how the choices compare:

| Option | What it fixes | What it does not fix |
|---|---|---|
| Seal the house | Stops bugs from getting past your walls and inside | Bugs still gather outside; does not change the food source |
| Remove the female boxelder | Cuts off your yard's main food source | Bugs can still fly in from a neighbor's tree |
| Replace with a male boxelder or another tree | No seeds to feed on from that tree going forward | Only helps for new plantings, not an existing female tree |
| Do nothing | No cost | Numbers tend to build year over year near an untreated seed source |

If the tree is otherwise healthy and you like the shade, sealing your house first is usually the cheaper, faster fix, and it works regardless of whose tree the bugs are actually coming from. Removal only makes sense as a bug fix on its own if the tree sits close to your home and the swarm is genuinely unmanageable. ## What Actually Works Against Boxelder Bugs

Sealing your house is the step that solves the problem no matter which yard the bugs came from. [UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/boxelder-bug/) recommends exclusion over spraying, noting that insecticides are generally no more effective than sealing entry points and clearing debris near the foundation.

A few things that help:

- Caulk cracks around windows, door frames, siding seams, and where utility lines enter the house, especially on south and west walls.
- Repair or replace torn window and door screens.
- Add door sweeps where daylight shows under exterior doors.
- Clear leaf litter, mulch, and debris in a 6 to 10 foot strip along the foundation, particularly on the sunny sides.
- Vacuum indoor bugs instead of crushing them, since crushing is what causes the stain and the smell.

None of that is tree work, and a heavy indoor infestation is really a pest control question rather than a tree question. If bugs are already inside your walls or living space in large numbers, a licensed pest control company can advise on interior treatment; that is outside what a tree service handles.

## Other Tree Pests You Might Be Confusing With Boxelder Bugs

Boxelder bugs get lumped in with other insects that actually do harm a tree, which is worth sorting out before you assume the worst. Aphids and scale insects feed on sap and can weaken a stressed tree over time, leaving sticky honeydew behind. Borers tunnel into wood and can kill branches or whole trees if left untreated. Boxelder bugs do none of that. They are a seasonal nuisance around the outside of your house, not a threat to the tree's health. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do boxelder bugs bite or spread disease?

No. They do not bite or sting, and they are not linked to spreading disease. That means it is safe to vacuum up a large indoor cluster yourself rather than calling anyone for safety reasons. Just avoid crushing them underfoot or on fabric, since that is what leaves a stain and a smell behind.

### What time of year are boxelder bugs the worst in Boise?

Fall is peak season, especially on warm, sunny afternoons after the weather starts cooling, when adults are actively looking for a place to overwinter. Some also reappear briefly in early spring as they leave their winter shelter, though the fall swarm is usually the bigger one.

### Will boxelder bugs keep coming back if I do not remove the tree?

Likely yes, as long as a seed-bearing female boxelder or maple keeps feeding them nearby, whether that tree is in your yard or a neighbor's. Sealing your house is what stops them from getting inside regardless of where the food source is.

### Are boxelder bugs the same thing as maple bugs?

Yes, "maple bug" is just a common nickname for the same insect. Boxelder trees are actually in the maple family, and the bugs will also feed on the seeds of some true maples, which is where the nickname comes from.

### Should I have my boxelder tree sprayed to kill the bugs?

Generally not worth it. Extension research has found that spraying insecticide on the tree is usually no more effective than sealing your house, so it adds cost without solving the underlying swarm. If bugs have already gotten inside in large numbers, that is a job for a licensed pest control company rather than tree spraying.

### Do I need to remove a healthy boxelder tree just because of the bugs?

Not necessarily. If the tree is otherwise healthy and you are fine with the seasonal seed drop, sealing your house solves the swarm without losing the tree. Removal is worth considering when the tree has its own problems, like weak or damaged limbs, decline, or a spot too close to your house.
