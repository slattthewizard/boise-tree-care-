# Bradford and Callery Pear Trees: Why They Split, and What to Do With Yours

Bradford pears split because their branches grow in tight, upright angles off the trunk instead of spreading out. At that narrow angle, bark gets pinched inside the joint rather than fusing into solid wood. Arborists call this included bark, and it does not get stronger as the tree grows. It gets weaker, while the limbs above it get heavier every year.

That is the short version of why a Bradford pear that looked perfectly healthy last summer can crack straight down the trunk during an ordinary thunderstorm, not even a major windstorm. If you are looking at a split trunk right now, here is the quick read: a young tree with no crack yet can sometimes be saved with structural pruning and cabling. A tree with a crack already through the trunk, or one that has already dropped a large limb, is usually past saving and should come down before it finishes the job on its own, onto your driveway, your fence, or your roof.

The rest of this covers why the tree grows this way, how to tell which stage yours is in, and what to plant if you decide to start over.

## Why Bradford Pears Split in the First Place

A healthy shade tree usually has one main trunk with branches that come off it at wide, angled unions, roughly the shape of an upper-case Y. Bradford pear does the opposite. It throws up several branches of nearly the same thickness, all close to the same height on the trunk, at steep angles closer to a V than a Y. [NC State Extension](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/callery-pear-bradford-pear-other-varieties-and-their-invasive-offspring) describes this as an "acute branching structure" that leads directly to weak branch unions, and calls the tree "notorious for easily breaking" in nothing worse than a moderate storm.

The mechanical problem is what is happening inside that V. Where two stems of similar size crowd together at a narrow angle, the bark of each one grows inward and gets trapped between them instead of the wood knitting together. [Clemson Cooperative Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/bradford-pear/) notes that this weak, splitting habit is one of the tree's defining flaws even though it remains a common landscape tree. Once that included bark is there, no amount of fertilizer or watering fixes it. The only ways to manage it are to remove the competing stems while the tree is young, or accept that the union will eventually fail.

## How Old Is Too Old for a Bradford Pear?

There is no fixed expiration date, but the pattern is consistent: the failures show up earlier than most homeowners expect, often well within the tree's first couple of decades, and usually get worse from there. A Bradford pear does not develop a stronger branch structure as it matures. It only adds more weight to a joint that was already the weak point on day one. That is why you will see plenty of 15 and 20 year old Bradford pears with a major limb already gone, or a visible seam running partway down the trunk.

If your tree is past that age and has never split, it has been lucky so far, not structurally sound. A calm year does not mean the next windstorm or heavy wet snow will go the same way.

## Warning Signs Your Pear Tree Is at Risk

Walk around the base and look up before you decide anything. Watch for:

- A visible crack or seam running down the trunk or a main branch union, even a hairline one
- An obvious V-shaped joint low on the trunk where two or more big limbs meet at a sharp angle
- A lopsided canopy, usually from a limb that already broke off in a past storm
- Bark that looks folded or pinched into a branch union instead of smooth and rounded
- New leaning after a windstorm, especially if the ground near the base has heaved or cracked
- Mushrooms, soft or crumbly bark, or a hollow sound when you knock on the lower trunk

## The Smell, the Thorns, and the Other Problems With Callery Pears

Splitting is not the only complaint homeowners have about this tree.

Bradford pear blossoms are showy but, as Clemson HGIC puts it plainly, they have an unpleasant fragrance. Most people who have one near a bedroom window or a patio notice it within the first bloom season and are not fans.

The Bradford cultivar itself is relatively resistant to fire blight, a bacterial disease that blackens blossoms and young shoots on pears and related trees, though resistant is not the same as immune. Other flowering pear cultivars sold alongside it, including Aristocrat and Redspire, are considerably more susceptible. If you are seeing blackened, scorched-looking branch tips on any pear or fruit tree on your property, see our guide to [fire blight in Boise](/blog/fire-blight-treatment-boise/) for what it is and what to do next.

Then there is the invasive offspring problem. The original Bradford was bred to be sterile on its own, but once nurseries planted other Callery pear cultivars nearby, cross-pollination became possible. Birds spread the resulting seeds, and the wild seedlings that come up are thorny, fast-spreading, and nothing like the tidy ornamental their parent trees were. That pattern has been serious enough that [several states, including Ohio and South Carolina, have moved to ban the sale](https://www.invasivespeciesinfo.gov/terrestrial/plants/callery-pear) of Callery pear varieties. Callery pear does not appear on Idaho's noxious weed list, but it is worth knowing what you are dealing with if seedlings are popping up in a fence line or empty lot near your property. ## Should You Save It or Remove It?

This mostly comes down to two questions: is there a crack through the trunk yet, and how much of the canopy is still healthy.

| Situation | Usual next step |
|---|---|
| Young tree, co-dominant stems, no crack | Structural pruning to remove one competing leader, sometimes cabling for support |
| Older tree, tight V unions, no crack yet | Cabling or bracing to reduce the load on the weak union |
| Crack already visible in the trunk or a major union | Removal, since the union will not heal or get stronger |
| Already dropped a large limb, canopy now lopsided | Removal, unless a certified arborist finds the remaining structure sound |

Cabling and bracing runs in the neighborhood of $200-$900 per system depending on the tree's size and how many unions need support, and it only makes sense before a crack has actually formed. Once wood has split, the joint is compromised for good and cabling just holds broken parts together rather than preventing a break. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Bradford pear trees banned in Idaho?

Ohio and South Carolina have banned the sale of Callery pear cultivars, including Bradford, but Callery pear does not appear on Idaho's noxious weed list, so nothing at the state level stops you from keeping or removing one. The concerns driving those bans, mainly invasive wild seedlings, are the same reasons many homeowners choose not to replant one here.

### How do I know if my tree is actually a Bradford pear?

Bradford pear and its relatives (Aristocrat, Cleveland Select, Chanticleer, and others) are all Callery pear cultivars and look similar: white spring blossoms, glossy oval leaves, and that upright, narrow branching pattern. If you are not sure which one you have, an on-site look from an arborist can confirm it and tell you how much structural risk your specific tree is carrying.

### Can a split Bradford pear be saved with cabling?

Cabling helps before a crack forms, by taking some of the load off a weak union. Once a union has already split, the wood is compromised there for good, and cabling mostly just holds broken pieces together rather than restoring strength. At that point removal is usually the safer and more cost-effective call.

### Do Bradford pear trees really smell that bad?

Most people think so. The white spring blossoms are attractive, but the fragrance is widely described as unpleasant, and it is strong enough that homeowners with one near a window or patio notice it every bloom season.

### Is it safe to remove a large Bradford pear myself?

Not if it has any lean, a visible crack, or is anywhere near a structure, fence, or power line. The same weak branch unions that cause spontaneous splitting also make the tree unpredictable to cut into, and a partially split trunk can fail without warning mid-cut. This is one of the clearer cases where hiring it out costs less than the alternative.

### What should I plant after removing a Bradford pear?

Look for a tree with a naturally strong single-leader structure rather than another Callery pear cultivar.
