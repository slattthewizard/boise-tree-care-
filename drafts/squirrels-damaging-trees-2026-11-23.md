# Squirrels Damaging Trees: When It's Harmless and When It Isn't

Squirrels damage trees in two main ways: they clip off twig tips, and they strip bark. The twig clipping looks messy but rarely hurts the tree. Bark stripping is the one to watch, because if a squirrel removes the bark all the way around a branch or trunk, everything above that point can die.

Most of the time the honest answer is "annoying, not dangerous." Here's how to tell the difference, why squirrels do it, and what you can actually do about it.

## What Squirrel Damage Looks Like

There are three things people usually find, and they're easy to mix up.

**Clipped twigs.** You'll see small branch tips, often with green leaves still attached, scattered under a tree. [The University of Maryland Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/squirrel-damage-trees-and-shrubs) says squirrels cut branches for nests, and that clipped twigs seldom damage the tree. The University of Nebraska-Lincoln's [NebGuide on tree squirrel damage](https://extensionpubs.unl.edu/publication/g1924/na/pdf/view) says twig clipping happens most often in spring and early fall.

**Stripped bark.** Patches of bark peeled off limbs, sometimes in long strips, with pale or damp wood showing underneath. Squirrels are chewing at the inner layer. This is the more serious one.

**Nests.** Big balls of leaves and twigs up in the branches, called dreys. The Maryland page says squirrels build them high in trees from sticks, leaves and stripped bark. The nest itself doesn't hurt the tree.

If you're not sure squirrels are the cause, the pattern helps. Squirrel-clipped tips have chewed ends and often green leaves still on them. A different problem, flagging, leaves brown, wilted leaves hanging on the branch. That points to something else, like disease or insects, and it's worth a separate look. A [tree health assessment](/blog/tree-health-assessment-boise/) will sort that out.

## Why Do Squirrels Chew Tree Bark?

Honestly, nobody has a settled answer. [Ohio State's Buckeye Yard and Garden Line](https://bygl.osu.edu/node/1466) says the exact cause is unresolved and that more research is needed. The ideas floating around are:

- They're eating the sugary inner bark (phloem).
- They're after sap or water.
- They're wearing down incisors that never stop growing.
- They need calcium in spring. Ohio State covers a study showing calcium rises in the phloem in spring and early summer, though a later small feeding trial weakened that idea.
- They want nesting material. Maryland notes that squirrels use stripped bark in their nests.

That article says stripping mostly happens from March through May and tends to be worse after a good nut year, when more young squirrels survive. The UNL guide says squirrels typically strip bark in winter and spring, and that smooth-barked deciduous trees take the most damage.

As for the clipped twigs, the reason isn't settled either. A [Wisconsin forest health note on oak branch tips](https://forestrynews.blogs.govdelivery.com/2018/09/26/oak-branch-tips-laying-on-the-ground-this-fall/) says squirrels seemed to clip branches partly to get at acorns more easily, though not every clipped branch had acorns on it.

## Which Trees Get Hit?

Smooth-barked deciduous trees are the usual targets, according to UNL. Ohio State lists honeylocust, maples and dawn redwood as trees where gray squirrels have stripped bark, sometimes up the trunk to 20 or 25 feet.

We don't have local numbers on which Treasure Valley trees get hit hardest, and we'd rather not guess. What we can say: if you have a maple, honey locust or similar smooth-barked tree with a squirrel problem nearby, those are the ones to check first.

One more detail from Ohio State that's worth knowing. Squirrels often focus on a single tree and leave the neighbors of the same species alone, so don't panic if only one tree is getting chewed.

## Cosmetic or Serious? How to Tell

Most squirrel damage falls on the cosmetic side. Here's a rough way to sort it.

**Probably cosmetic:**

- Clipped twig tips and leaves on the ground. UNL says trees can lose up to about 30 percent of their foliage without significant impact, and Wisconsin forest specialists said clipped oaks generally recover completely.
- Partial bark stripping that doesn't go around the whole branch. UNL puts it at damage up to about 50 percent of the trunk's circumference without significant impacts.

**Worth worrying about:**

- Bark removed all the way around a branch or trunk (girdling). Everything above the ring is cut off from food moving down from the leaves. A girdled branch will leaf out poorly or wilt, then die.
- A branch with fresh stripping that suddenly has dead or brown foliage beyond it.
- Stripping on a young tree with a thin trunk, since there's less bark to lose before the ring is complete.
- Wounds that stay open and weep, or that show fungus or insect holes.

If you find a girdled limb, it generally has to come off. A girdled branch over a path, driveway or roof is also a safety issue. See our guide to [removing limbs and branches](/blog/tree-limb-branch-removal-boise/) if you're deciding what to cut. If a main trunk is half chewed, that's a case for having an ISA-certified arborist look at it.

## What You Can Do About Squirrels Damaging Trees

There's no easy fix, and a lot of what's sold is hit or miss. Here's what the extension sources say is worth doing.

**Do nothing, at first.** For clipped twigs and light stripping on a healthy mature tree, tolerating it is a perfectly good plan. Maryland says little control is needed for clipped twigs.

**Protect young or favorite trunks.** UNL describes metal collars at least 2 feet wide, placed 6 to 8 feet above the ground, with the edges overlapped and connected by springs so the collar can expand as the tree grows. A collar only works if squirrels can't jump onto the trunk from a nearby branch, wire or roof. UNL also mentions petroleum-based repellents for tree access, with a plastic barrier between the repellent and the bark and following the label. For young trees that deer and voles chew too, see our piece on [protecting young trees from deer and voles](/blog/deer-vole-protection-young-trees/).

**Cut off the highways.** UNL says to trim limbs at least 8 feet away from buildings so squirrels can't jump to the roof. [UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/tree-squirrels/) also suggests cutting back trees that overhang roofs or sit close to wires. If your tree is already crowding the house, our [guide to trees too close to your house](/blog/tree-too-close-to-house-boise/) covers when trimming is enough and when it isn't. Branch clearance is a standard [tree trimming](/tree-trimming-boise/) job, and it helps with more than squirrels.

**Remove the food.** Maryland recommends removing food sources like bird feeders as a first step. We know that's not a popular one.

**Seal the building, not the tree.** If squirrels are getting into your attic, that's a building problem. UC IPM's page suggests blocking gaps under eaves, overlapping roof sections and knotholes with sheet metal or 1/4-inch wire hardware cloth, after making sure nothing is still inside. That's a job for a roofer or wildlife contractor, not a tree crew.

We aren't going to tell you what's allowed for trapping or relocating squirrels here, because rules depend on the agency and the situation. If you're considering it, check with the state wildlife agency first.

## Squirrels Nesting in Hollows and Trunk Holes

Squirrels also den in tree cavities. A hole with a squirrel in it isn't automatically a sign the tree is failing, but a cavity big enough to den in can mean the wood inside is decayed. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do squirrels chew tree bark?

Nobody knows for certain. Extension researchers point to several possibilities: eating the sugary inner bark, getting sap or water, wearing down their constantly growing teeth, taking in calcium in spring, and collecting nesting material. Different squirrels may be doing it for different reasons.

### Will squirrels kill my tree?

Rarely. Clipped twigs and partial bark stripping usually don't. The real danger is girdling, where bark is stripped completely around a branch or trunk. That kills the branch above the ring, and on a thin young trunk it can kill the whole tree. Most healthy mature trees come through fine.

### How can I tell squirrel damage from disease?

Squirrel clipping leaves chewed tips with green leaves often still attached, plus debris on the ground. Disease and insect problems tend to leave brown, wilted leaves that stay on the branch. Stripped bark shows fresh, chewed wood. If you can't tell, a tree health assessment will sort it out.

### What's the best way to stop squirrels chewing bark?

Physical barriers work better than anything else. A smooth metal collar about 2 feet wide, placed 6 to 8 feet up the trunk, keeps squirrels from climbing, as long as they can't jump in from a nearby limb. Trimming limbs back from buildings and removing bird feeders also helps. Labeled repellents are a backup, not a fix.

### Should I trim branches near my house because of squirrels?

Yes, it's a good idea. UNL recommends keeping limbs at least 8 feet from buildings so squirrels can't jump onto the roof. That also reduces damage from branches rubbing your roof and gutters. Trimming should be done cleanly, back to a branch or trunk, not as a topping cut.

### Is it a problem if squirrels nest in my tree?

A leaf nest on a branch is harmless. A squirrel living in a cavity can be a sign the trunk or limb is hollow, and that needs a closer look. If the tree is otherwise sound, it may be fine, but it's worth having someone check how much solid wood is left.
