## What a tree cutting service actually covers

"Tree cutting" is a homeowner phrase, not a trade term. Crews use narrower words, and knowing them will save you money because it stops you paying for the bigger version of the job.

**Pruning or trimming.** Cutting selected branches off a tree you intend to keep. This is clearance work over a roof, deadwood removal, thinning a crown so wind passes through it, or lifting low limbs off a lawn. See [tree trimming in Boise](/tree-trimming-boise/) for what a trim visit includes.

**Removal.** Taking the entire tree down, trunk and all. Full [tree removal in Boise](/tree-removal-boise/) is a different insurance and rigging problem from trimming, which is why the price jumps.

**Sectional dismantling.** Taking a tree down in pieces from the top instead of felling it in one drop. In most Boise yards there is nowhere safe to lay a 60 foot trunk, so this is the normal method rather than the exception. The difference is explained in [felling versus sectional dismantling](/blog/tree-felling-vs-sectional-dismantling/).

**Emergency limb work.** Getting a broken branch off a roof, fence, car or driveway after a windstorm. It is priced by risk and access, not by the size of the branch.

**Stump grinding.** Chewing the leftover stump below grade with a machine. It is a separate job from the removal and is often quoted as a line item, which is why a "cheap" removal quote sometimes leaves a stump in your lawn.

A single crew usually does all five. A single price does not.

## What a tree cutting service costs in the Treasure Valley

Here is the honest version of the price question. Every figure below is a typical range, not a quote, and the real number moves with height, lean, decay, distance from the house and whether a truck can get near the tree.

| Job | What it means in plain words | Typical range |
|---|---|---|
| Trimming or pruning | Cutting selected branches off a tree you are keeping | $150 to $2,000+ by tree size |
| Removal | Taking the whole tree down, trunk included | $300 to $5,000+ by height |
| Emergency limb work | Getting a broken limb off a structure | $150 to $850 |
| Tree on a structure | A whole tree down on a house, garage or fence | $1,500 to $4,000+ |
| Stump grinding | Grinding the stump below ground level | $75 to $400 per stump |
| Health assessment | An arborist inspecting before you decide | $75 to $250, often waived if work is booked |

Removal splits cleanly by height. Under 30 feet is $300 to $800. Between 30 and 60 feet is $800 to $2,500. Over 60 feet is $2,500 to $5,000 and up, and an 80 foot tree that needs a crane or sits in a hazard position starts around $5,000.

Two things to watch on any quote. Stump grinding added to a removal usually runs $150 to $500, and hauling the wood away may or may not be included. Ask about both before you compare numbers.

## Who to hire for tree cutting work in Boise

The job titles are not interchangeable, and the gap between them shows up in your yard.

**An ISA-certified arborist** has passed the International Society of Arboriculture exam and keeps the credential current with continuing education. That is the credential worth asking about, and you can read what it means at [treesaregood.org](https://www.treesaregood.org/), the ISA's own consumer site. An arborist is who you want when the question is "is this tree safe" or "can this tree be saved," not just "can you cut it."

**A tree cutting crew** is the production side: climbers, ground crew, chipper, rigging. Plenty of excellent crews work under a certified arborist who assesses the tree and writes the plan. That combination is the normal setup for good companies.

**A landscaper with a chainsaw** is a different risk category. Trimming a shrub and dismantling a 60 foot tree over a roof are not the same skill, and general landscaping insurance frequently excludes tree work at height. If a tree job goes wrong on your property and the contractor is not covered for it, the claim can end up pointed at your homeowner policy.

The title question comes up constantly, and [tree surgeon versus arborist](/blog/tree-surgeon-vs-arborist/) unpacks the rest of the vocabulary.

## Work a tree cutting service should not touch

Some jobs are not yours to hire out, and a crew that agrees to them anyway is telling you something.

**Anything touching a power line.** Vegetation on distribution lines in this valley is handled by [Idaho Power](https://www.idahopower.com/), not by a private crew and not by you. Do not hire anyone who offers to trim a branch off a live line, and do not go up a ladder to look. Call Idaho Power and let them clear it first. [Trees near power lines in Boise](/blog/tree-near-power-lines-boise/) explains where the utility's responsibility starts and stops.

**Street trees and permit-regulated removals.** Boise runs a street-tree permit regime, and the tree between the sidewalk and the curb is often not yours to cut even though you mow around it. Nobody should tell you removal is permit-free without checking. Start with [tree removal permits in Boise](/blog/tree-removal-permits-boise/) before you book anything near the street.

**Structural questions that need an engineer.** If a tree has failed onto a load-bearing part of your house, or roots are suspected in a foundation problem, the tree crew handles the tree and a qualified engineer handles the structure. Those are two separate visits and two separate scopes of work.

**Anything a crew has to guess at.** If a tree is hollow, cracked at the union, or leaning after a storm, the honest answer is often that it needs an on-site assessment before anyone quotes. Site conditions decide these jobs, and a firm price given over the phone for a tree nobody has seen is a guess wearing a number.

## How to compare two or three quotes

Most homeowners in this market call two or three crews. That is the right move, as long as you are comparing the same job.

Ask each company to write down five things: the exact trees and the exact work, whether the stump is included, whether the wood and brush are hauled away, whether they carry liability insurance and for how much, and who on the crew holds the ISA credential.

Then compare. If one quote is dramatically lower, it is usually missing the stump, missing the cleanup, or missing the insurance. That is not a bargain, it is a different job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a tree cutting service the same as tree removal?

No. Removal is one of the jobs a tree cutting service does, along with trimming, emergency limb work and stump grinding. Removal means the whole tree comes out, trunk included, and it is the most expensive item on the list. If you only need branches cleared off a roof, ask for trimming and you will pay a fraction of the removal price.

### How much does a tree cutting service cost in Boise?

It depends on which job you are actually buying. Trimming typically runs $150 to $2,000 or more by tree size, removal runs $300 to $5,000 and up by height, and stump grinding runs $75 to $400 per stump. Those are typical ranges, and the real figure moves with access, lean, decay and how close the tree sits to a building. The [cost calculator](/cost-calculator/) will narrow it down for your tree.

### Does the price include grinding the stump and hauling the wood?

Often not, and that is where quotes stop matching each other. Stump grinding added to a removal usually runs $150 to $500, and some crews price debris hauling separately. Ask both questions out loud before you compare two numbers, because a quote without them is not the finished job.

### Can a tree cutting service trim a branch off my power line?

No. Vegetation touching distribution lines is Idaho Power's responsibility, and no private crew should be working on a live line at your house. Call Idaho Power and let them clear the conductor first. Once the line is dealt with, a tree crew can handle the rest of the tree normally.
