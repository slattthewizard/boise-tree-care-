# What Is Your Tree Worth? Tree Appraisal Explained

That 60-year-old oak shading your front yard might be worth more than your car. We are not exaggerating. When a construction crew damages a mature tree's roots, when a storm takes one down, or when a neighbor's excavator clips a trunk during a fence project, the question everyone eventually asks is some version of how much is a mature tree worth, and the honest answer is: usually a lot more than people expect, and it takes an actual formula to figure out.

We get asked to weigh in on tree value more often than you would think, usually because an insurance adjuster offered a number that felt too low, or because a real estate deal or property line dispute suddenly needs a dollar figure attached to a tree that has been standing for decades. Here is how that number actually gets calculated, and why it is rarely as simple as "look up a price online."

## Why Anyone Needs a Number for a Tree

A tree does not usually have a market price the way a car or a piece of furniture does, but plenty of situations force the question anyway.

**Insurance claims** come up after storm damage, vehicle impact, or a neighboring property's work damages roots or a trunk. **Construction or contractor damage disputes** happen when excavation, grading, or utility work injures or kills a tree that was not supposed to be touched. **Real estate transactions** sometimes need a tree's value assessed, particularly for casualty loss documentation or estate purposes. **Neighbor and boundary disputes** occasionally end up needing a neutral, defensible number when a tree near a property line is damaged or removed without permission, which connects closely to the liability questions we cover in [tree fell on fence responsibility in Idaho](/blog/tree-fell-on-fence-responsibility-idaho/).

In nearly all of these, "just replace it with a similar tree from the nursery" does not actually work, because you cannot buy a 60-year-old oak at a garden center. That gap between what a sapling costs and what a mature tree is actually worth is exactly what a formal appraisal is meant to capture.

## How Arborists Actually Calculate Tree Value

Tree appraisal is a recognized discipline within arboriculture, with published methodology used by consulting arborists nationwide. Without getting into the exact math (which varies by method and is genuinely something a trained appraiser should run, not something to eyeball), the general approach works like this.

**Trunk size matters most.** Larger trunk diameter generally means a higher base value, since trunk size roughly correlates with the decades of growth that would be needed to replace the tree.

**Species class affects the multiplier.** Some species are valued higher because they are longer-lived, more structurally sound, or simply more desirable as landscape trees. A fast-growing, weak-wooded species with a short functional lifespan is valued lower than a strong, long-lived species of the same size.

**Condition adjusts the number down (or occasionally confirms it).** A tree in excellent health with no structural defects retains close to its full calculated value. A tree with disease, decay, storm damage, or poor structure gets a reduced valuation, sometimes substantially.

**Location and function matter too.** A tree providing meaningful shade to a home, screening from a busy street, or anchoring a landscape design is valued differently than a similar tree in an out-of-the-way corner of a lot with little functional or aesthetic contribution.

This is often referred to loosely as a trunk formula approach, and it is the industry-standard concept behind most tree appraisals, though the exact calculation involves published cost tables and adjustment factors that a qualified appraiser applies rather than a simple formula you can run yourself. If you have seen mentions of the Council of Tree and Landscape Appraisers' guide, that is the general framework most consulting arborists reference. [University of Idaho Extension](https://www.uidaho.edu/extension) also publishes general resources on tree value and landscape economics that can help frame the conversation before you bring in a specialist.

## Why a Big Shade Tree Can Be Worth Five Figures

This surprises people every time. A young 2-inch caliper tree from a nursery might cost $200 to $400 installed. A mature 30-inch diameter shade tree, the kind that took 50 or 60 years to grow, cannot be replaced at any price with an equivalent nursery purchase; trees that size simply are not sold that way. Appraisers account for that reality by calculating what it would functionally cost to replace the tree's contribution, not by pricing out an impossible like-for-like swap. The result, for a genuinely large, healthy, well-placed tree, often lands well into five figures.

## Rough Value Ranges: What to Expect

These numbers are meant only as a general sense of scale. A formal appraisal is the only way to get a defensible number for an actual claim or dispute.

| Trunk Diameter (measured at 4.5 ft) | Rough Condition | Typical Appraised Value Range |
|---|---|---|
| 4-8 inches | Good health, common species | $500-$2,000 |
| 10-16 inches | Good health, desirable species | $2,000-$6,000 |
| 18-24 inches | Good health, mature specimen | $5,000-$12,000 |
| 26+ inches | Excellent health, prime specimen, prominent location | $10,000-$25,000+ |

Condition problems, poor species value, or a tree in a low-visibility spot can push any of these ranges down significantly. These figures are hedged on purpose. Actual value depends heavily on species, exact condition, and local factors an appraiser evaluates in person.

## What Lowers a Tree's Appraised Value

Not every mature tree appraises for a large number. Several factors bring the figure down.

**Disease or pest damage** in progress, particularly something already reducing canopy or structural integrity, lowers value proportionally to severity. **Structural defects** like included bark, major cavities, or a significant lean reduce both the tree's expected remaining lifespan and its appraised worth. **Poor species selection** for the site, an aggressive, short-lived, or weak-wooded species, caps the value regardless of size. **Prior topping or severe improper pruning** can permanently limit a tree's value even if it survives, since the structural damage rarely fully heals.

## Hiring an Arborist to Do a Formal Appraisal

For anything involving real money, an insurance claim, a legal dispute, or a significant construction damage case, you want a consulting arborist with specific credentials in tree appraisal, not just a general tree service estimate. Look for someone who can produce a written report following recognized appraisal methodology, since insurers and courts generally expect documentation that follows an established standard rather than an informal opinion. Our [ISA-certified arborists](/about/) can point you toward the right resource for a formal appraisal, and if the tree's condition itself is in question first, a [tree health assessment](/blog/tree-health-assessment-boise/) is often the right starting point before a full valuation.

A good appraisal report typically includes species identification, trunk measurements taken at the standard height, a written condition assessment covering health and structural defects, photos documenting the tree's condition, and a clearly explained valuation method rather than just a bottom-line number. That level of detail is what holds up when an adjuster or an opposing party in a dispute pushes back on the figure. A verbal opinion, even from a knowledgeable arborist, rarely carries the same weight once a claim moves past an initial conversation.

**A scenario from Harris Ranch:** A homeowner's blue spruce, roughly 22 inches in trunk diameter and a defining feature of the front yard, had its root zone significantly damaged when a neighboring property's construction crew ran heavy equipment too close during a home addition. The homeowner's initial conversation with the neighbor's contractor offered a token amount toward "landscaping." A formal arborist appraisal, accounting for the tree's size, species, condition prior to the damage, and its role in the landscape, came back at just over $9,000, which became the basis for the eventual settlement.

**A scenario from the North End:** After a severe windstorm took down a large, decades-old maple in a front yard, the homeowner's insurance company initially offered $500, the standard limit many policies place on "landscaping" claims without documentation. An arborist appraisal established the tree's pre-storm value at roughly $6,500 based on its size, species, and prominent role shading the house, which supported a supplemental claim beyond the standard landscaping cap. Insurance specifics vary a great deal by policy, so always confirm your own coverage limits and documentation requirements directly with your insurer.

## What This Means If You Are in the Middle of a Dispute Right Now

If you are currently dealing with tree damage from a storm, a neighbor's project, or a contractor's mistake, document everything before any cleanup happens: photos of the tree from multiple angles, the trunk diameter if you can measure it safely, and anything showing the tree's condition before the damage. That documentation makes a much stronger case for a later appraisal than trying to reconstruct the tree's condition from memory. If the situation is heading toward an insurance claim tied to storm or structural damage, our guide on [tree fell on your house insurance in Idaho](/blog/tree-fell-on-house-insurance-idaho/) covers the claims process side of things.

It also helps to write down what you remember about the tree even if you cannot photograph it in its original state: roughly how tall it was, how long it had been on the property, and any past maintenance you had done on it. Contractors, neighbors, or insurers on the other side of a dispute rarely have an incentive to argue for a higher value, so the burden of building the record tends to fall on the property owner. Acting quickly, before memories fade and before the site changes further, puts you in a much stronger position when the appraisal conversation actually happens.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a tree appraisal the same as a regular tree service estimate?** No. A tree service estimate quotes the cost of work like removal or trimming. A tree appraisal establishes the monetary value of the tree itself, typically for insurance, legal, or real estate purposes, and follows a different, more formal methodology.

**How much does a formal tree appraisal cost?** Costs vary based on the complexity of the situation and whether a written report suitable for insurance or legal use is required. It is worth asking upfront what level of documentation you need for your specific situation, since a quick opinion costs less than a formal, litigation-ready report.

**Can I appraise my own tree using an online calculator?** Online tools can give a rough starting estimate, but they cannot account for your specific tree's condition, species value in your region, or site-specific factors, so they are not reliable for an actual claim or dispute.

**Does a diseased or declining tree still have appraisal value?** Usually some value, but significantly less than a healthy tree of the same size. The appraisal accounts for reduced remaining lifespan and diminished structural or aesthetic contribution.

**Who pays for a tree appraisal in an insurance claim?** This depends on your policy and the situation. Sometimes the homeowner requests and pays for an independent appraisal to support a claim; in disputed situations, cost allocation can become part of the negotiation. Check your specific policy or consult with an attorney for legal disputes.

**What information does an arborist need to appraise a tree?** Typically trunk diameter, species, an in-person condition assessment, and photos of the site showing the tree's location and role in the landscape. For damage claims, photos from before the damage occurred are extremely valuable if available.

**How does tree value relate to arborist consultation costs generally?** Appraisal work is a specialized service distinct from routine maintenance pricing; our general guide on [arborist costs in Boise](/blog/arborist-cost-boise/) covers standard consultation and service pricing if you are trying to understand the broader range of what arborist visits typically cost.
