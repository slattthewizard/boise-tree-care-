# Do You Get a Discount for Removing Multiple Trees at Once in Boise?

Yes, in most cases. Batching several trees into one visit typically brings the per-tree cost down compared to paying for separate service calls, because one crew's setup gets split across multiple jobs instead of billed against a single tree.

What you will not get from any honest company is a guaranteed flat percentage off. Every tree on the property still carries its own size, access, and hazard variables that get priced individually, and that is the real logic behind any multiple tree removal discount Boise homeowners get quoted. It helps to understand the mechanics before you assume a set number is coming.

## The Quick Answer

| Situation | What usually happens |
|---|---|
| One tree, one visit | Full mobilization cost is carried by that single job |
| Several trees, same property, same visit | Mobilization cost splits across all of them; per-tree pricing often improves |
| Trees at different addresses or scheduled on different days | Little to no savings; each stop still needs its own setup |
| Multiple stumps ground in one visit | The most consistent volume discount category, since the grinder is already running |
| One large tree plus several small ones | Savings are real but smaller in percentage terms, since the large tree's labor dominates the total |

## How the Mobilization Cost Actually Works

Every tree job starts before anyone touches a chainsaw. A crew loads the truck and chipper, drives from the shop to the property, walks the site, and confirms access and hazards. When the work is done, someone hauls the debris away and cleans up. None of that is cutting time, but all of it is real cost.

On a single medium tree, priced in the $800 to $2,500 range for a 30 to 60 foot removal, a meaningful slice of that number covers getting the crew and equipment there and hauling everything out afterward, not just the cutting itself. Add a second and third tree at the same address that same day, and the crew does not drive back to the shop and reload between jobs. That fixed slice gets divided by three jobs instead of one.

This is not unique to tree work. Any trade that has to show up before it can start, electricians, plumbers, excavation crews, prices a version of this same setup cost into every visit.

## What Changes, and What Does Not, When You Batch Trees

The part that gets cheaper is the shared part: one trip, one setup, one cleanup and haul-out. The part that does not change is the tree-specific labor. A small tree under 30 feet still runs $300 to $800. A medium tree, 30 to 60 feet, still runs $800 to $2,500. A large tree over 60 feet still runs $2,500 to $5,000 or more, and anything requiring a crane or extreme access still runs $5,000 to $8,000 or more. Access, proximity to a structure, and how much debris has to be hauled out still get priced per tree, batched or not.

**A homeowner in Meridian** had three medium ash trees killed by emerald ash borer along a shared back fence line and wanted all three down before storm season. Priced as three separate calls, each tree would have carried its own $800 to $2,500 range plus its own trip charge. Quoted together in one visit, the combined total came to $5,400, noticeably less than three separate calls would have added up to near the top of that range, because the crew set up once, worked through all three, and hauled everything out in a single trip.

So a multiple tree removal discount is really a discount on the shared cost of showing up, not a discount on the tree-specific work. That distinction matters when you are comparing quotes.

## Where a Multiple Tree Removal Discount Shows Up Most: Stump Grinding

Stump grinding is the category where volume pricing is most consistent. A grinder has to be delivered, positioned, and set up whether it grinds one stump or six. Once it is on site and running, moving it from stump to stump costs mostly time and fuel rather than a new setup.

Canonical stump grinding pricing runs $75 to $150 for stumps under 12 inches, $150 to $300 for 12 to 24 inches, and $250 to $600 or more for anything larger, with a $75 to $400 headline range per stump. Volume discounts on multiple stumps ground in one visit are standard, because the marginal cost of one more stump once the machine is already there is small compared to the cost of bringing it back a second time.

**A property in Garden City** had four stumps left from a windstorm the previous winter, ranging from a 10 inch ornamental to a 22 inch shade tree stump. Ground individually across separate visits, those would have priced somewhere across the $75 to $300 tiers each, plus a machine delivery fee every time. Ground together in one afternoon, the whole job came to $620, well under what four separate visits would have totaled, since the grinder made one trip instead of four.

## Why No Company Can Honestly Promise a Fixed Percentage

Ask a contractor for "20 percent off for three trees" and a careful one will hesitate, and that hesitation is a good sign, not a red flag. Size mix, access, disposal volume, whether stumps are included, and hazard proximity to a structure all vary job to job. A fixed percentage would either overprice the easy jobs or underprice the hard ones, and neither is sustainable pricing.

A more useful question than "what's my discount" is "what does batching these together do to the total compared to separate visits." That question gets you an itemized answer instead of a marketing number.

It is also worth knowing that batching trees into one visit does not change the permit picture. Boise's street-tree permit rules apply per qualifying tree, regardless of how many are scheduled in the same day. If any of the trees sit in the public right-of-way or on a boulevard strip, each one still needs its own application through the city, something our guide on [tree removal permits in Boise](/blog/tree-removal-permits-boise/) walks through in more detail. The [City of Boise](https://www.cityofboise.org) publishes the current permit requirements for street trees if you want to confirm before scheduling.

None of this changes the safety math, either. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a tree company give me an exact discount percentage before seeing the trees?

No honest crew will commit to a fixed percentage sight unseen, because size, access, and hazard level all change the math tree by tree. What they can do is walk the property, price each tree individually, then show you the combined total so you can see how much reflects shared setup cost versus tree-specific labor. That itemized approach protects you better than a vague promised discount would.

### Does batching save more on removal or on stump grinding?

Stump grinding is usually the more consistent place to see the benefit, since a grinder already running on site can move from stump to stump for mostly the cost of time and fuel. Removal savings are real too, but one very large tree can dominate the invoice enough that the shared mobilization saving looks smaller by comparison.

### If I have trees at two different addresses, does the discount still apply?

Rarely, because the crew still has to load out, drive, and set up separately at each address, which is exactly the cost that batching is meant to avoid. Two trees on the same property, or on adjacent properties visited back to back, are a different story, since the truck and crew only travel once either way.

### Do I still need a permit for each tree if I am removing several at once?

Yes. Boise's street-tree permit rules apply per qualifying tree no matter how many are scheduled in the same visit, so batching changes the price math, not the permit requirement. Any tree in the public right-of-way or on a boulevard strip still needs its own application before removal.

### Is it worth calling more than one company to compare multi-tree pricing?

It is worth getting at least one detailed, itemized quote so you understand what you are actually paying for shared costs versus tree-specific work, and a second quote for comparison is reasonable. What is not useful is asking each company for a flat "percentage off" before they have seen the property, since that number means little without the itemized breakdown behind it.
