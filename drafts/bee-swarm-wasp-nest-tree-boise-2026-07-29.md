# Bee Swarm or Wasp Nest in a Tree You Need Worked On?

A honeybee swarm in a tree needs a beekeeper, never a can of spray, and the tree work waits until the colony is safely gone. A wasp or hornet nest is usually a simpler fix: a small one often gets cleared the same visit, while a large, aggressive nest means rescheduling a few days so pest control can treat it first. Either way, a bee swarm or wasp nest in a tree changes the plan for your Boise removal or trim, and knowing which one you have tells you what happens next.

## Honeybee Swarms: Call a Beekeeper, Not an Exterminator

A swarm is a temporary cluster, usually a few thousand bees resting on a branch for a day or two while scout bees search for a permanent home. Swarming honeybees are typically calm since they have no hive or brood to defend yet, but that calm is exactly why they get mistaken for something safe to ignore or spray. Neither is true.

Honeybees aren't native to Idaho, but they still do a lot of the pollination work in Treasure Valley yards and orchards, and local beekeeping groups will usually collect a swarm free or for a modest fee rather than see it killed. [University of Idaho Extension](https://www.uidaho.edu/extension) has homeowner resources on protecting pollinators if you want the fuller reasoning behind never reaching for insecticide on a swarm.

Our rule is simple. If a swarm or an active colony shows up on a job site, work on that tree stops, no cutting, no climbing, no spraying, until a beekeeper has safely removed it. A swarm hanging in the open is usually the faster pickup. A colony already living inside a cavity is a cutout, which takes longer to schedule since the beekeeper has to open the cavity to get the comb and bees out intact.

**A scenario from Meridian:** A homeowner scheduled removal of a 45-foot cottonwood with a shallow lean that needed to come down before it dropped a limb on the fence. Our crew arrived, started the pre-cut assessment, and found an active honeybee colony living inside a trunk cavity about 15 feet up. The $2,100 removal, already quoted, went on hold for 12 days while a local beekeeper performed a cutout and relocated the colony. Once the bees were gone, the crew came back and finished the job at the original price.

Trying to handle a cavity like that yourself, including anything living inside it before you cut into the trunk, is exactly the kind of risk we cover in [DIY tree removal versus hiring a professional](/blog/diy-tree-removal-vs-professional/).

## Wasps and Hornets: Usually a Faster Fix

Paper wasps build small, open, umbrella-shaped nests under eaves or tucked in a canopy, and they're generally not aggressive unless the nest itself gets disturbed. Bald-faced hornets and yellow jackets are a different animal: bigger, enclosed, papery nests, sometimes the size of a volleyball or larger by late summer, and a far more defensive colony guarding it.

Because we're licensed and insured, our ISA-certified crew makes the call on site rather than guessing from the ground. (You can see what that credential actually requires at [isa-arbor.com](https://www.isa-arbor.com).) A small paper wasp nest well clear of the cut zone often gets knocked down and cleared as part of the same visit.

A large or aggressive nest, especially one hidden in the exact section of canopy we need to cut, means we stop and ask you to schedule pest control treatment first. We don't spray nests ourselves as an add-on. Cutting into a treated branch safely afterward is still tree work, and treating the nest itself is a pest control company's job, not ours.

**A scenario from Garden City:** A property called for deadwood removal on a large black walnut before it dropped brittle limbs on a fence. Mid-job, the crew found a bald-faced hornet nest about the size of a volleyball wedged in a fork exactly where the next cut needed to go. Work stopped that afternoon. The homeowner had a pest control company treat the nest two days later, and the crew returned to finish the $420 deadwood job at the price originally quoted.

A hornet nest hidden high in a canopy near a power line is one more reason we won't send anyone up there. ## How a Bee Swarm or Wasp Nest in a Tree Delays (or Doesn't Delay) the Job

Whether the job on the calendar is a full [tree removal](/tree-removal-boise/) or routine [tree trimming](/tree-trimming-boise/), a nest or swarm changes the timeline about the same way:

- **Small paper wasp nest** away from the cut zone: little to no delay.
- **Hornet or yellow jacket nest** that needs treatment: usually a few days while pest control schedules the visit.
- **Honeybee swarm**: a few days to about two weeks depending on beekeeper availability, longer during peak swarm season in spring and early summer.
- **Honeybee cutout** from inside a cavity: the longest wait, since it's a more involved job for the beekeeper.

## When to Stop and Call a Professional

A few situations are worth flagging before you touch anything yourself:

- Any nest or hive inside a tree you're planning to cut above shoulder height on your own.
- Anything within reach of a power line. That's Idaho Power's territory, not a homeowner's or a tree crew's; their downed-line number is (208) 388-2323.
- Climbing a ladder to inspect or knock down a nest or hive, even a small one.
- A tree with an active nest that's already leaning on, or could reach, a structure. Our 24/7 emergency line still applies here. Call and we'll get a crew out the same day, working carefully around the nest until it's cleared through our [emergency tree service](/emergency-tree-service-boise/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do you spray honeybee swarms?
No. Honeybees do enough pollination work in Treasure Valley yards and orchards that local beekeepers would rather collect a swarm than see it killed, often free or for a modest fee. Our crew stops work on the tree until the swarm or colony is safely relocated, and we can point you toward local beekeeping contacts if you don't already have one.

### Will a wasp nest delay my scheduled tree trimming or removal?
It depends on the nest. A small paper wasp nest away from the section we're cutting is often cleared the same visit, while a large bald-faced hornet or yellow jacket nest, especially one hidden inside the part of the tree we need to work on, means pausing for a few days until pest control treats it.

### Who do I call first, a beekeeper or an exterminator?
Honeybees always go to a beekeeper, never an exterminator, since the goal is relocating the colony rather than killing it. Wasps, hornets, and yellow jackets are a pest control company's job. If you're not sure which insect you have, a photo through our contact form or a quick description over the phone usually settles it.

### Does finding a nest or swarm change the price of my tree removal or trimming?
Usually not for the tree work itself, since our pricing is based on the size and complexity of the tree, not what's living in it. The beekeeper's or pest control company's fee is separate and billed by them directly, not folded into our quote.

### Is it safe to knock down a wasp nest myself before your crew arrives?
We'd rather you didn't, especially if the nest is more than a few feet off the ground or anywhere near where a chainsaw will be running. A miscalculated hit on an active hornet nest from a ladder is a common way people get stung repeatedly or fall, so it's worth leaving the timing to us or to a pest control company.
