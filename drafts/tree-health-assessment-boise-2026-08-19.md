## What an Arborist Actually Looks At

A real assessment is not a walk-by. It works from the ground up, because most tree failures begin below eye level.

**The root collar and root zone.** Where the trunk flares into the ground is the single most informative spot on a tree. A trunk that enters the soil like a telephone pole, with no visible flare, usually means the tree is planted too deep or buried by grade changes, and that slowly strangles it. Girdling roots wrapping the trunk do the same thing, and our guide on [how to fix girdling roots](/blog/girdling-roots-fix/) covers what can be done. Soil compaction from parking, construction traffic, or heavy foot use also shows up here, and [soil compaction and tree roots](/blog/soil-compaction-tree-roots/) explains why it matters more than most homeowners expect.

**The trunk and main stems.** Cavities, seams, bulges, missing bark, fungal conks, and included bark where two stems meet. A [crack in a tree trunk](/blog/crack-in-tree-trunk/) is read differently depending on where it is and whether it's closing or opening. Fungal fruiting bodies at the base are among the more serious findings, because they usually indicate decay that has been progressing for years.

**The canopy.** Density compared to what the species should carry, deadwood in the upper crown, dieback starting at branch tips, leaf size and color, and whether growth has slowed. Tip dieback at the top of a tree often means a root problem, not a branch problem.

**Leaf and needle condition.** [Leaves turning yellow in summer](/blog/tree-leaves-turning-yellow-summer/) is a common trigger for calls, and in the Treasure Valley the cause is frequently chlorosis from our alkaline soils rather than disease. Reading that correctly saves a lot of money.

**Pests and pathogens.** [Emerald ash borer](/blog/emerald-ash-borer-idaho/), [fire blight](/blog/fire-blight-treatment-boise/), [codling moth](/blog/codling-moth-fruit-trees-boise/), borers, and the [tree diseases common in Boise](/blog/tree-diseases-boise/) each leave distinct signatures.

**Site and history.** Irrigation pattern, recent construction, grade changes, what was planted where, and what the neighbors' trees are doing. Context explains more decline in this valley than any single symptom does.

## When a Health Assessment Is Worth Paying For

**The tree changed suddenly.** A tree that leafed out normally in May and thinned dramatically by July is telling you something happened at the roots. Sudden change is more diagnostic than slow decline.

**Storm damage that isn't obvious.** After a windstorm, the visible broken limbs are the easy part. Root plate movement, a trunk that shifted, and cracks that opened are what get missed. Our [windstorm tree damage checklist](/blog/windstorm-tree-damage-checklist/) covers the immediate steps, and an assessment is the follow-up.

**Construction is coming or just finished.** Trenching, grading, and equipment traffic within the root zone cause decline that surfaces one to three years later. [Protecting trees during construction](/blog/protect-trees-during-construction/) is the preventive version; an assessment afterward tells you what survived.

**You're buying or selling.** [Buying a home with mature trees](/blog/buying-home-mature-trees-boise/) covers why a large tree near a house is a material item, and Idaho sellers should read [tree disclosure obligations](/blog/selling-home-tree-disclosure-idaho/).

**A tree is near a target.** A structure, a driveway, a play area, a neighbor's roof. [A leaning tree](/blog/leaning-tree-dangerous-boise/) or a [hollow tree](/blog/hollow-tree-safe-or-hazard/) near something valuable deserves a real look rather than a guess.

**Someone already quoted you a removal.** This is the highest-value case. A second opinion on a **$2,500 to $5,000+** removal costs **$75 to $250** and sometimes changes the answer entirely.

## What Happens After the Assessment

Four outcomes, roughly in order of frequency.

**Monitor.** The tree is stressed but stable, and the correct action is a watering or mulching change plus a look again next season. [How to water trees in Boise](/blog/how-to-water-trees-boise/) and [how to mulch around trees](/blog/how-to-mulch-around-trees/) are where most of these end up, because deep infrequent watering and a proper mulch ring fix more Treasure Valley tree problems than any product does.

**Treat.** Fertilization for a genuine deficiency, chelated iron for chlorosis, or a targeted pest response. Note that [fertilizing trees in Boise](/blog/fertilize-trees-boise/) is not a default recommendation; feeding a tree with a root problem does nothing.

**Prune.** Structural pruning, deadwood removal at **$150 to $800**, or [crown thinning, raising, or reduction](/blog/crown-thinning-raising-reduction/) at **$250 to $1,500**. Never topping, for the reasons in [why tree topping is bad](/blog/tree-topping-bad-boise/).

**Support or remove.** Cabling and bracing at **$200 to $900** per system can extend the life of a structurally flawed but otherwise healthy tree. Where the finding is advanced decay in the trunk or root plate, removal is the honest answer, and our [tree removal](/tree-removal-boise/) page covers the process and pricing tiers.

## How to Vet Whoever Does the Assessment

This matters more than for most tree work, because you are buying a judgment rather than a task.

- **ISA certification.** Ask for the certification and verify it through the [International Society of Arboriculture](https://www.isa-arbor.com/) directory. Anyone can call themselves an arborist; the credential is checkable.
- **Liability insurance, with a $2 million minimum.** Ask for a certificate of insurance sent directly from the insurer, not a photocopy. Verify workers' compensation coverage as well, because on an uninsured crew an injury on your property can become your problem.
- **No pressure toward removal.** A company whose assessment always concludes "remove it" is selling removals, not assessments.
- **A written finding.** Even a short one. Verbal conclusions don't help you with an insurer, a buyer, or a second opinion.
- **Separate the assessment from the sales call.** Free estimates are how you price work you already know you need. An assessment is different, and paying for it buys you an opinion that isn't attached to a job.

If you need something more formal, for a permit application, an insurance claim, a dispute with a neighbor, or a legal matter, that's a written arborist report rather than an assessment. [When you need an arborist report](/blog/arborist-report-when-you-need-one-boise/) covers the difference and when the formal document is required.

Be especially careful after a storm. [Storm chaser scams](/blog/tree-service-storm-chaser-scams-boise/) reliably appear in Boise after high-wind events, and door-knocked "free inspections" that immediately find an emergency are the standard opening.

## What's Different About Assessing Trees in the Treasure Valley

Local conditions produce a distinct set of findings, and an arborist who works elsewhere will misread some of them.

**Alkaline soils.** High pH soil across the valley locks up iron, and the resulting chlorosis produces yellow leaves with green veins, most visibly on maples, and it's frequently mistaken for disease or overwatering. The fix is soil chemistry, not a chainsaw.

**Irrigation mismatch.** Lawn sprinklers deliver shallow, frequent water, which is close to the opposite of what an established tree needs. A great many "dying" trees here are trees with roots that never went deep because they never had to. [Drought-stressed trees in Boise](/blog/drought-stress-trees-boise/) covers the other end of that.

**Canal and ditch water.** Properties on gravity irrigation have seasonal water availability, and trees adapted to a summer canal schedule respond badly when the delivery changes. See [trees near irrigation canals and ditches](/blog/irrigation-canal-ditch-trees-boise/).

**Species mismatch.** A lot of the valley's older plantings are species that were never suited to this climate or soil. Our guide to the [worst trees to plant in Boise](/blog/worst-trees-to-plant-boise/) covers the recurring offenders, and best trees to plant in Boise covers what actually thrives here.

**Winter injury.** [Frost cracks and sunscald](/blog/frost-cracks-sunscald-trees/) show up on thin-barked young trees on south and west exposures, and the damage appears in spring, months after the cause.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a tree health assessment cost in Boise?

**$75 to $250**, depending on the number of trees and the complexity of what's being evaluated. That fee is often waived if you schedule work as a result of the visit. A formal written arborist report for permit, insurance, or legal purposes is a separate and more expensive product.

### Can you tell if a tree is dying just by looking at it?

Sometimes, but the confident cases are the extremes. A tree with no leaves in July is finished; a tree with full dense canopy is fine. Everything between those needs the root collar, trunk, and canopy read together, because canopy thinning can mean a root problem, a pest, a soil chemistry issue, or a watering pattern, and those have very different answers.

### Is a tree health assessment the same as an arborist report?

No. An assessment is a diagnostic visit that ends in a recommendation. An arborist report is a formal written document prepared for a third party, like a city permit office, an insurer, or a court. If someone is going to read it who wasn't on site, you need the report.

### When is the best time to assess a tree in Boise?

The growing season, roughly May through September, because a tree in leaf shows you what it can and can't support. Dormant-season assessment is still useful for structural evaluation, since branch architecture, cracks, and decay are easier to see without foliage, but decline is harder to judge.

### Should I get a second opinion before removing a tree?

If the tree is healthy enough that the answer isn't obvious, yes. A removal in the **$800 to $5,000+** range is worth a **$75 to $250** second look, particularly if the first opinion came from someone who knocked on your door.
