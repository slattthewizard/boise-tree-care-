## License and Insurance: What to Actually Verify

"Licensed and insured" gets printed on every truck door, so the words alone mean nothing. What matters is whether the crew can produce a certificate of insurance with a carrier name and a policy number, and whether that carrier confirms the policy is active when you call it directly, not a number the crew hands you.

This step matters more after a storm than any other time of year. A falling limb, a dropped section of trunk, or a ladder against the siding can cause real damage in seconds, and if the crew has no active insurance, any damage they cause becomes your bill, not theirs. Idaho homeowner policies typically cover $500 to $1,000 per tree for storm-related removal, up to $5,000 per storm event, and that coverage protects you from the storm, not from a contractor's mistake on top of it.

A few other signs tend to show up together on a bad crew, and any one of them is worth pausing on. Full payment upfront, in cash, before any work starts is one. Skipping the permit on a street tree is another: Boise has a street-tree permit process, and a crew that removes one from the parking strip without pulling a permit can leave the homeowner holding the liability, not them. A crew willing to cut anything touching a power line is a third. [Idaho Power](https://www.idahopower.com) handles all vegetation work on distribution lines, and their number, (208) 388-2323, is who to call for anything touching an active wire. A crew that offers to cut it themselves is either unlicensed for that specialty work or improvising dangerously.

If a crew claims ISA-certified arborists are on staff, [the International Society of Arboriculture's credentials page](https://www.isa-arbor.com/Credentials) explains exactly what that certification requires, so you know what you're actually being told. A crew that balks at naming the certified individual, or says there isn't time because "the tree could go any minute," is telling you what you need to know.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if a tree service is actually licensed and insured?
Ask to see a certificate of insurance listing the carrier's name and a policy number, then call that carrier directly to confirm the policy is currently active. Don't rely on a number the crew hands you on the spot. A legitimate company will not hesitate to let you verify this before any work starts.

### Why do so many tree crews show up right after a storm?
A single windstorm can create hundreds of jobs across the Treasure Valley in one day, and that surge attracts crews who travel from storm to storm rather than serving one community year-round. Local companies also get busy, but they have a fixed address and a reputation to protect here next month, which a traveling crew doesn't.

### Is it normal to pay a deposit before storm cleanup work starts?
A modest deposit tied to a signed, written estimate is normal. Being asked for full payment in cash before any work begins is not, and it's one of the clearest signs the crew doesn't intend to finish the job or stand behind it.

### What should I do if a tree is touching a power line after a storm?
Do not let any tree crew touch it. Call Idaho Power at (208) 388-2323, since they handle all vegetation work on distribution lines, and stay well clear of the tree and any wires until a crew has responded.

### Can a storm chaser legally remove a street tree without a permit?
No. Boise's street-tree permit process still applies right after a storm, and a crew that skips it can leave the homeowner responsible for the violation even though the crew did the actual cutting. Ask any crew working on a parking-strip tree whether a permit has been pulled before they start.
